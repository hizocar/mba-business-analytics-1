# Program

**Business Analytics I · From Python to Nonlinear Models** is the first of two modules on data and AI for managers. It runs over six weeks: each week has a **live session of 4 hours** and a **self-paced lab of about 2 hours**, for 24 hours live and 12 hours self-paced.

## Who it is for

Managers and professionals who make decisions with data but do not program, or have not programmed in years. The module asks for no mathematics beyond percentages and averages. It explains every model with pictures and simulators first, and with code second; the code is always written for you.

## What you will be able to do

By the end of the module you can:

- open, run, and adjust a Python notebook in Google Colab, and read code well enough to know what it does;
- clean a business dataset, summarize it with tables and charts, and turn what you see into findings;
- build and evaluate a **regression** model to estimate a number (a price, a cost, a sales figure) and explain its drivers;
- build and evaluate a **classification** model to estimate a risk (a cancellation, a default, a churn), and choose a decision threshold with business costs;
- recognize when straight lines are not enough and use **decision trees, random forests, and gradient boosting**;
- compare models fairly, explain what drives their predictions, and present a recommendation to a management audience.

Each **live session** has two blocks of about 100 minutes with a break: the first explains the idea with simulators and live examples; the second applies it to Altamar's data and ends with a **case clinic** where teams take the same step with their own case.

Each **self-paced lab** is a notebook that runs top to bottom as it is. You change the settings marked ✏️, rerun, and answer the questions in the notebook. It ends with a **graded quiz** of five questions, due before the next live session.

## Tools

Everything runs in the **browser**: each session page has its code ready to run (press ▶), with nothing to install. For the applied case you will use **Google Colab**, which saves your work and lets your team share it. The [Colab guide](colab.md) explains how to start.
